01

THE SHORT VERSION / ~15 SEC

Return to triathlon.
Make an Excel sheet.

One of my favourite people inspired me to get back into triathlon.

So, naturally, I made an Excel sheet to track the comeback.

Then the sheet connected to other things I had been learning and building. AI became useful. Bikes became interesting in a new way. A 3D model needed somewhere to live. The room became a collection. The collection wanted a world.

Now there is an island.

There was no grand master plan. Just the next interesting puzzle.

One bike. One room. One road. One problem. The whole picture did not need to be clear. Only the next piece.

Somewhere along the way, the pieces started fitting together. That became Kona.m: a place to explore triathlon through identity, machines, stories, places and the small details that make the sport feel alive.

03

THE UNFILTERED VERSION

Side quest.
Side quest. Side quest.

A completely unnecessary detail can become absolutely essential if you stare at it long enough.

Normal response: start training.

My response: build the spreadsheet. Then improve the spreadsheet. Then learn the systems around it. Then ask what a bike would look like as data, geometry, 3D and interaction.

That kept happening. A technical question became a visual question. A visual question became a room. A room became a world. A world needed stories, objects, progression and doors.

Some of the detours failed. Some were abandoned. Some only made sense much later. But the useful pattern was simple: learn one thing, build one thing, discover the next thing, connect ideas that were not supposed to sit next to each other.

THE PART THAT MATTERS TO ME

Finished enough to let other people in.

Not finished-finished. Not a declaration that every next step is known. Just a point where scattered pieces became coherent enough to share, test and keep improving in public.
