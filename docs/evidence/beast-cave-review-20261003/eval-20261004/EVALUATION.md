## 2. If I were Lionel Sanders

> "OK. Somebody actually did their homework. Louisville 2010, the 29th in 2016, Arizona six weeks later, second in 2017, the hour. That's the arc, and it's told as a loop instead of a trophy case. *The work nobody sees*: yeah, that's the right sentence. The 2:27 slab is a bit of a gut-punch, but it's honest.
>
> But it isn't my room. A pain cave doesn't look like a design studio with acoustic foam and a neon sign. Part of the point of the pain cave was that it's ugly. The bike isn't my bike and the trainer is generic. That's fine *as long as nobody thinks it's mine*, and right now a fan absolutely would.
>
> And why does my room have a door that says THROUGH HERE · BREITLING? I don't have a deal with them. Same with the Zwift box: 'Build this cave' with prices sitting next to my name reads like I'm selling it.
>
> The ride thing, holding a power band for sixty seconds while the fans spin up, is the part I'd actually show my audience. That's the idea. Make that the room."

**Why I think that's the reaction**

1. **It's accurate, and that earns trust.** Every number on the wall comes from published results, with sources a tap away. Athletes notice immediately when a fan project gets facts wrong. This one doesn't.
2. **The concept matches the public persona.** Self-experimentation, honesty about failure, the loop of trying again. "Not a trophy room" lands. The Gap is the most emotional object in the room, which is a risk, but it's a fair one.
3. **The look is cinematic but generic.** The neon, the foam and the clean concrete read as "gaming-streamer studio", not as a real pain cave. The authenticity he's known for is exactly what the art direction smooths over.
4. **Association risk is the real objection.** The Breitling link door and the Zwift price card sit inside *his* room. The disclaimers say "unofficial", but the placement says partner. For any athlete with real sponsors, that alone is a no.
5. **The interaction is the hook.** A 60-second interval you physically hold is shareable and audience-shaped. That's the part that makes it *for* him, not *about* him.

## 3. If I were his manager or agent

> "Nice work, and we can't let it go public like this. Name, story and brand adjacency without an agreement is our problem, however good the disclaimers are. Three things before we'd even take a call:
> 1. **No third-party brands inside the athlete room.** Take out the Breitling door and the Zwift price card. If there's ever a hardware partner, it's *his* partner and it's negotiated.
> 2. **An approval path.** He signs off on the facts, the copy ('The island answered back' is cute; he may not want it) and the tone.
> 3. **What's in it for him?** A room that drives his audience somewhere they can ride his interval, with his channel linked and maybe a leaderboard. Not a museum exhibit about him."

## 5. If I were a first-time KONA.m visitor (the audience you'd be sending him)

> "The home page is gorgeous and I get it in one line. Then I'm asked why I'm here, then I'm building a voxel guy, and the 3D world 'opens later from Discover'. Where's the cool room from the video? The jokes are fun once, and they're the first thing I read."

**What that tells us**
- **The room can't be reached from the public journey.** It's review-only, and even the public 3D world is gated behind progression and Discover. A pitch that ends on "open this link" needs a direct, ungated deep link to land in the room.
- **The tone is playful.** "Expensive hobbies", "dreaming irresponsibly" and "Who are we sending into the lava?" are charming for amateurs, but they sit oddly next to a sincere athlete story. Consider a calmer variant when someone arrives through an athlete link.
- **Phone defect:** the *Why Kona* pill overlaps onboarding headings (`app-phone-race-self.png`). The browser-blue "What is Kona.m?" link also breaks the visual system on the home screen.

**Overall:** *intrigued, not yet a yes.* It's good enough to start a conversation. It isn't yet something he'd share without changes.

## 7. The five changes that would turn "intrigued" into "I'd post this"

1. **Remove adjacency.** Drop the Breitling link door and sign, and remove the Zwift price card from the athlete room. Keep the Zwift hardware data for a neutral "Gear" page or a future negotiated partner slot.
2. **Rough it up.** Less design studio, more lived-in: exposed pipes, a cheap rug, worn foam, a bare-bulb option, a cluttered shelf. Ask his team what the real one looks like rather than guessing. Keep the cinematic lighting and lose the polish.
3. **Make the interval the product.** A shareable result card ("I held 43 s of the Beast Interval"), a public leaderboard, and a "Lionel's line" ghost trace that appears only once he supplies or approves a real session.
4. **A direct, ungated deep link.** Something like `/beast-cave` that lands in the room after one tap, with a calm athlete-mode intro instead of the joke onboarding.
5. **An approval kit for his team.** A one-page PDF with the facts and sources, a list of every on-screen sentence, what's generated versus original, and the rights boundaries. Signed-off copy replaces anything he doesn't like.
