# Would this room get you flown to Kona to work with Lionel Sanders? (2026-10-04)

**Short answer: no. Not on its own, and not yet.** This is an honest read, not a guess at anyone's real words.

## Why not

1. **A fan room is a gift, not a hire.** Athletes get tribute projects every week. A team flies someone to Kona when that person solves a problem the team already has. Examples: more reach, a sponsor deliverable, content made during race week, or a product the audience pays for. Today the room shows that you can build. It doesn't yet show what it does *for Lionel*.
2. **There's no proof of audience.** Nobody has ridden the interval yet. There is no leaderboard, no shared result cards and no numbers. "Look what I made" is weaker than "4,000 of your fans rode your interval this week."
3. **It isn't public.** The room sits on a review branch behind `?reviewRoom=beast-cave`. Until it runs at a stable URL on a phone in one tap, Lionel's team can't forward it to anyone.
4. **There's no relationship.** Nobody on Lionel's team knows you yet. Cold outreach turns into work through a small paid or unpaid trial, rarely through an invite to Kona.
5. **Rights cap what it can be.** No likeness, no Zwift footage, no sponsor marks. That's correct, but it means the room can never show Lionel himself until his team chooses to be in it.
6. **Kona logistics.** Race-week travel, credentials and housing get planned months ahead. Whoever travels is budgeted inside the team's sponsor plan.

## What would

| Step | What it proves | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Merge and ship the room at a clean public link (`/beast-cave`), with the calm athlete intro | They can open it on a phone in one tap | KONA.m (you) |
| 2. Promote **Zwift's Big Weekend ride, Tue 6 Oct 23:00 UTC** from the room *(done in this build: TV countdown, door poster, intro card, calendar file, result card, share image)* | The room sends people *to Lionel's own event* | Done |
| 3. A public interval leaderboard plus share cards, then post: "ride the Beast Interval, then ride with Lionel on Zwift" | Real numbers: riders, shares, clicks to his event | You |
| 4. One short email or DM to his team: the link, the approval kit, one stat, one ask ("may we keep this up, and would Lionel ride the interval once?") | Respect, no surprises, and an easy yes | You |
| 5. Offer a concrete race-week deliverable, e.g. a "Kona 2026 race-week room" updated daily, or interactive content for his channel | It solves *their* problem in the busiest week | You and his team |
| 6. If they say yes: Lionel rides the interval, so "Lionel's line" goes live with his approved copy, and optionally a licensed Zwift clip on the TV | The room becomes *his* | His team |

**The realistic path:** first a "yes, keep it up", then "yes, we'll share it", then a small paid piece of work, and only then an invite to Kona. The room is a good door-opener for the first step. Steps 1, 3 and 4 are the ones that move it.
